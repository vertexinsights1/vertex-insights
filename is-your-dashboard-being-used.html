How to tell whether your dashboard is really being used

Many dashboards are built, admired, and then quietly abandoned. The signs are easy to spot if you know where to look.

Signs of trouble

People still ask for the numbers by email, or in a spreadsheet. Meetings begin with someone reading figures aloud from a different file. Nobody can say who owns a number when it looks wrong. The dashboard shows thirty measures and nobody can name the three that matter.

Three questions to ask

Which decision did this dashboard change last month? Who opens it every week, and what do they do next? When did someone last challenge a number? If the answers are vague, the problem is usually design, not technology.

What good looks like

A small number of measures, agreed with the people who use them. Each has an owner, a written definition and a target. The data refreshes on a schedule people trust, and there is a short review rhythm: weekly for operations, monthly for finance.

Start small

Five measures that are used beat thirty that are not. Look at the dashboard again after three months and remove anything nobody opens.
