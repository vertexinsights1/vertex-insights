Budget, forecast or scenario: which one do you actually need?

People use these three words as if they were interchangeable. They are not. Each answers a different question, and choosing the wrong one is why many plans are written once and never opened again.

A budget is a plan

It sets out what you intend to earn and spend over a year, agreed in advance. Its job is to set targets and give managers something to be measured against. Its weakness is that it ages: by month four, conditions may have moved on.

A forecast is an estimate

It states where you expect to finish the year, given what you know now. It is usually refreshed monthly as results arrive. It does not replace the budget. The gap between the two is the useful signal.

A scenario asks “what if?”

Keep the same model and change one assumption: material costs up 8%, your largest customer lost, a 5% price rise with some volume lost. Scenarios do not predict. They show how exposed the business is and which decisions have room.

Which to use

If you have no budget, start there. A simple one beats none. If you have a budget nobody opens after March, add a monthly forecast. If you face a specific decision, such as a price change, a hire or a loan, build scenarios around that decision.

One rule for all three

Keep the assumptions visible on a single sheet. A model nobody can inspect is a model nobody will trust.
