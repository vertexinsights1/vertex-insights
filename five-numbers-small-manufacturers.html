Five numbers every small manufacturer should see every month

Most owners know last month’s sales. Fewer can say, without a phone call, which products earned money, whether orders left on time, or how long customers take to pay. Five measures cover most of what matters, and each can be built from records you already keep.

Gross margin by product

Total margin hides the difference between a product that earns well and one that only keeps machines busy. Calculate it from the selling price less material, direct labour and direct processing cost. Review it every month, and again whenever input prices move.

On-time delivery

The share of orders dispatched by the promised date. Track it by customer if a few customers account for much of your sales. A falling figure usually shows up here well before it shows up in complaints.

Order backlog and lead time

How much work is waiting, and how long an order takes from confirmation to dispatch. Together they show whether you are short of capacity or simply scheduling badly.

Receivable days

How long, on average, customers take to pay. Profitable firms run short of cash when this number drifts upward, so it deserves its own line in the monthly pack.

Rework and scrap rate

The share of output that is redone or thrown away. It is a direct cost, and it points to specific machines, shifts or suppliers.

Putting them to use

Place the five on one page with last month, the same month last year, and a target you have chosen. Add one line of comment per measure. If a measure never leads to a question or an action, remove it.
